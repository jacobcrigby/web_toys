How it works

The key idea: volt-second balance

An inductor resists changes in current: its current ramps at a rate of VL / L. Positive VL → current rises; negative → it falls.

In steady state the current must end each cycle where it started, so the area under the VL trace above zero (switch on) must equal the area below zero (switch off). That single fact fixes the output voltage — watch the red VL trace, and try changing D while the sim runs to see the areas fall out of balance until Vout catches up.

The capacitor smooths what's left of the ripple. Make L small or the load light (high R) and the inductor current will hit zero every cycle — discontinuous mode — where the simple formula stops being true.
